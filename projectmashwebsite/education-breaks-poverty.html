Education & Poverty

Education: The Breaker of Poverty in Our Communities

December 2025 Mathare, Nairobi

"Education is the most powerful weapon which you can use to change the world." Fifty years of economic research backs that line up with hard numbers — and Sub-Saharan Africa shows the single biggest payoff on the planet.

Economists have been measuring the "returns to schooling" for half a century, and the finding has barely moved: each additional year of education raises a person's earnings by roughly 9–10% on average, worldwide. What's less widely known is where that return is highest. According to World Bank research spanning 139 countries, the payoff per extra year of schooling is greatest in Sub-Saharan Africa, at around 13.5% — well above the global average, and higher than in North America, Europe, or East Asia.

Zoom out further and the scale gets almost hard to grasp. UNESCO estimates that if every adult worldwide completed just two more years of school, nearly 60 million people could escape poverty. If every adult finished secondary school, the figure climbs to 420 million people lifted out of poverty — more than the entire population of the United States.

Why It Stays Stuck in Communities Like Ours

So if education is this reliably profitable, why doesn't every family invest in it? Because in communities like Mathare, the obstacle has never been the value of education — it's the price of the door. Even where tuition is technically free, the real costs of uniforms, books, exam fees, and the income a child could otherwise be earning add up to more than many households can absorb in a given month. Poverty doesn't make education less valuable; it makes the upfront cost of reaching it harder to pay, even when the return on the other side is enormous.

"The return on education in Sub-Saharan Africa is the highest in the world. The barrier was never the value of learning — it was who could afford the door."

That's the gap YouthRise Changemakers exists to close — covering the supplies, the space, the mentorship, and the small costs that quietly keep capable children out of classrooms. Every shilling that removes one of those barriers isn't charity in the traditional sense. By the World Bank's own numbers, it's one of the highest-yield investments available anywhere on earth — and the dividend is paid out in a Mathare child's entire working life.

Sources: World Bank, 50 Years After Landmark Study, Returns to Education Remain Strong (2024); World Bank, Education & Skills overview; UNESCO/Global Partnership for Education poverty-reduction estimates, cited via Global Citizen.
