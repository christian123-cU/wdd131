Gender Equality

Why Educating Girls in Mathare Doubles Impact

October 2025 Mathare, Nairobi

UNESCO estimates over two million secondary school girls drop out worldwide every year. In Nairobi's informal settlements, the gap between boys' and girls' attendance is one of the starkest in the country. Here's why closing it pays off twice over.

Across Kenya, the numbers tell a clear story about where the system is leaking. Nationally, girls' primary school enrollment is actually slightly higher than boys' — but the gap reverses sharply at secondary level, and it reverses hardest among the poorest households. UNICEF data shows that among Kenya's poorest families, secondary attendance sits at roughly 33% for boys and 25% for girls — a wider gender gap than anywhere else in the income distribution. In Nairobi's own informal settlements, including Kibra and Mathare, researchers have documented some of the country's highest rates of girls leaving school before completing secondary education.

The drivers are well documented: early pregnancy, child marriage, the cost of school fees and supplies, and unpaid domestic labor at home all weigh more heavily on girls than boys in low-income settlements. UNICEF's research on child marriage in the region found that 62% of women first married before age 18 had no education at all — compared to just 8% of those who'd completed at least secondary school. The pattern runs in both directions: a girl out of school is at far higher risk of early marriage, and early marriage is one of the strongest predictors of never returning to school.

Why It Pays Off Twice

This is where the economics get interesting. The Global Partnership for Education's research is blunt about the multiplier effect: educated girls go on to have fewer, healthier, and better-educated children themselves. Put simply — invest in a girl's education today, and the dividend doesn't stop with her; it compounds into the next generation before she's even finished raising one.

"In Mathare, 43% of girls don't attend school at all, compared to 29% of boys. That gap isn't fixed — it's a choice we make every time we decide who gets support to stay in class."

That's the thinking behind our Girls Club, sanitary towel distribution, and mentorship programs at YouthRise Changemakers — practical interventions aimed squarely at the reasons girls actually leave school in Mathare, not abstract ones. Every girl who stays enrolled through secondary school isn't just one statistic improved. She's the start of a different statistic for her own children.

Sources: UNESCO, education dropout reporting (cited in Kenyatta University research on Kibra/Langata, Nairobi); UNICEF Kenya, Education country page; UNICEF, Child Marriage in Eastern and Southern Africa (2022); Global Partnership for Education, girls' education research.
