Roots and Rise: The Literacy Crisis We Can't Afford to Ignore

Programme Spotlight Mathare, Nairobi

How would you feel spending two years in ECDE and eight years in primary school — only to leave unable to read a simple sentence or follow a basic paragraph?

That's not a hypothetical. It's the daily reality for the majority of children moving through Kenya's primary system — and it's exactly the crisis Roots and Rise, our Foundational Literacy and Numeracy (FLN) programme, was built to stop.

According to Uwezo, the East African learning-assessment body, a 2021 national assessment found that 60% of Grade 4 learners could not read a Grade 3-appropriate text — children who had already passed the grade where that skill is supposed to be locked in. An earlier Uwezo assessment found that as far back as 2014, roughly one in every ten children completed primary school without having fully mastered Class 2-level literacy and numeracy. Lost learning that takes root in a child's earliest years rarely closes on its own in the years that follow — it compounds, grade after grade, until a learner is sitting in a Form 2 classroom unable to do what a Grade 2 curriculum expected of them.

Why Mathare Feels This Harder

This national gap is not evenly spread — and Mathare sits squarely where it bites hardest. Overcrowded classrooms push teacher-to-learner ratios far past what any single teacher can manage individually. Many children begin school having had no ECDE preparation at all, walking straight into Grade 1 already behind. Lessons taught only in English or Swahili can leave a child who thinks in their home language locked out of content from day one. And a child trying to concentrate on an empty stomach is fighting biology, not laziness.

None of these are obscure, fringe problems. They are the most common, well-documented drivers of learning poverty across Kenya's informal settlements — and they stack on top of one another, child after child, classroom after classroom.

"Roots speaks to the foundational skills every child needs before anything else can grow. Rise is what happens once those roots are finally in place."

What Roots and Rise Does Differently

Roots and Rise tackles this as one connected problem, not three separate ones. It trains teachers in ability-based instruction methods aligned with the Teaching at the Right Level (TaRL) approach championed by TaRL Africa. It groups children by what they can actually do, not just their age, so a Grade 4 learner reading at a Grade 1 level gets Grade 1-appropriate support instead of being carried along regardless. It feeds every child before the lesson starts, because concentration has to be fed before it can be taught. And it extends literacy and numeracy support to the parents and guardians those children go home to — because a child who masters reading at school but returns to a home with no literate adult is still missing half the support system research shows actually sustains the gain.

This isn't a guess at what might work. It's the same evidence base used by TaRL Africa, Uwezo Kenya, and the PAL Network across multiple African countries, adapted specifically for Mathare's classrooms, languages, and households.

Sources: Uwezo / Teaching at the Right Level Africa, Kenya needs to invest in advancing literacy and numeracy skills in early grades (2021 national learning assessment); Uwezo Kenya annual learning assessment reporting (2014).
